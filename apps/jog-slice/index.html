Lens marks
focus
A lens motor knows only how far along the barrel it is — 0 to 100% of travel. Marks turn that into real distances, the same way an AC marks the barrel. Without marks the lane still works and still repeats; it just reads in percent, and the 3D export leaves focus out rather than inventing a distance.

A stepper is open loop, so Calibrate is what makes a position mean anything: it drives the barrel into both stops and measures the travel between them. The board refuses to run a pull on an axis it has not calibrated since it powered up.
Connection doctor
A handshake that times out looks the same whether the cable is charge-only, the NMX is unpowered or in BLE mode, the wrong port was picked, or the device address is not 3. This asks every address and counts what came back, so those stop looking alike.
Try these, in order
    Do the taught limits still mean anything?
    What the controller said
    A taught limit is a step count, and a step count only means a place on the rail relative to the controller's origin. If the NMX power-cycles without restoring position, that origin resets while these numbers do not — so the guard rail moves somewhere else while you still believe in it. Enforcing that is worse than enforcing nothing, which is why a voided limit reads as untaught: the axis goes back to creep speed, and a slow collision is a noise.
    Recorded passes
    Every pass is recorded while it runs: the controller's own percent, and each motor's reported position, sampled on the same poll that drives the progress bar. Passes are compared to each other at matched percent, never at matched wall-clock — two takes started three seconds apart are still the same move at 40%. Readings taken while a motor was mid send-to are set aside rather than mixed in: the firmware silently quarter-steps during a send and rescales what it reports.

    Every pass is written to disk the moment it ends, so a restart does not cost you the session:
    Compare two passes
    A whole-percent progress report cannot resolve motion finer than one percent of the path, so a result at or under that floor is reported as a bound — "repeats to at least this" — not as a measurement. The five-pass tape check in docs/HARDWARE-BRINGUP.md still measures the endpoint better than this does; this measures everything in between, which the tape cannot.
    Rig commissioning
    The NMX reports motor steps and has no encoder, so nothing in software can know how far a step moves the carriage — that is your belts, pulleys and microstep setting. Until it is measured, a 3D export is a shape, not a camera move. Measure once per mechanical configuration, and again after changing a belt, a pulley or a driver jumper.

    Tier 1 fires cues from this computer: roughly ±20 ms and not repeatable between passes — fine for a cue light, not for anything the audience will see A/B'd in a composite.
